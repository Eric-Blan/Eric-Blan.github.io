Website Evaluations

UNC Charlotte - Well Designed

  • Contrast: UNC Charlotte's website uses its brand colors — green, white, and gold — with strong intentionality. Dark green headers and navigation elements contrast cleanly against white content areas, while bold photography of campus and students provides vivid visual contrast against structured text blocks. Important calls-to-action like "Apply Now" stand out immediately without competing with surrounding content.
  • Repetition: The site maintains a consistent visual identity throughout. The same UNC Charlotte green appears in navigation bars, section headers, and button elements across every page. Card-based layouts for news stories, academic programs, and events follow identical structural patterns, giving the entire site a cohesive, professionally unified feel that reinforces the university's brand.
  • Alignment: Content is organized along a clear grid system. Navigation links, hero text, and feature cards all snap to consistent columns and margins. The hierarchy of headline, subheading, and body copy is predictable and readable. Nothing feels out of place or arbitrarily positioned, which creates a sense of institutional credibility and polish.
  • Proximity: Related content is clustered into clearly defined sections with adequate whitespace between them. A news story card groups its image, headline, date, and read-more link tightly together as one unit. Admissions links, program finders, and contact information are each contained in their own logical zones, making it easy to find what you are looking for without hunting across the page.

Craigslist - Poorly Designed

  • Contrast: Craigslist offers almost no meaningful contrast. The entire site is plain black hyperlinks on a white background with zero use of color, imagery, or visual weight to establish hierarchy. Every category and link looks exactly the same, making it impossible for the eye to determine what is most important or where to look first.
  • Repetition: The site repeats one thing obsessively — blue hyperlinks — but with no variation or purpose. There are no consistent card styles, button designs, or visual components that create a design system. The repetition that does exist works against the user by making every item feel equally important, which ultimately means nothing stands out at all.
  • Alignment: The homepage is a dense wall of left-aligned text columns with minimal padding and no grid structure holding them together. Column widths are inconsistent, and the relationship between category groups is communicated only through proximity and font size rather than any visual alignment system. The result looks less like a web page and more like a plaintext document.
  • Proximity: While categories are technically grouped by topic, the groupings are communicated purely through small bold labels with no visual separation — no borders, background colors, or whitespace to reinforce the boundaries. Dozens of unrelated links are crammed into the same visual space, forcing users to read laboriously through every item rather than scanning recognizable content zones.
